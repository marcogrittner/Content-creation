# 4. YouTube Long-Form Scripts

## The YouTube video structure (use it for every video)

```
0:00–0:30   COLD OPEN / HOOK   Show the most exciting moment first, then state the promise of the video.
0:30–1:00   SETUP              Who you are (one sentence), what we're doing today, why it matters to the viewer.
1:00–X      MAIN CONTENT       Chapters. Each chapter opens with a mini hook ("but then something went wrong…").
                               A "pattern interrupt" every 60 to 90 seconds: B-roll, a graphic, a joke, a location change.
X–end-1:00  PAYOFF / CLIMAX    The result, the reveal, the emotion.
Last 30s    OUTRO              The lesson + "watch this next video" (end screen). No long goodbyes.
```

**Sponsor read placement:** right after the setup (about 1:00 to 2:30), 45 to 60 seconds, introduced naturally.

**Gear you need (a simple setup):** an iPhone/Android with good stabilization, a GoPro or Insta360 (chest/bar mount), a small wireless mic (DJI Mic / Rode Wireless), and a cheap tripod. Later: a drone (check local rules) and a mirrorless camera.

## Video 1 – "I Ate Like a Pro Cyclist for 24 Hours (7,000 kcal)"
**Length:** 12 to 15 min | **Pillar:** Food | **Thumbnail:** You behind a mountain of pasta looking shocked. Text: "7,000 KCAL"

**[0:00 – COLD OPEN]**
> *(B-roll: a massive pasta plate hitting the table, you riding in the rain, a counter animation "0 → 7,000 kcal")*
> **SAY:** "Today I'm going to eat 7,000 calories. Not as a challenge, not for fun. Because if I don't, my body literally can't train tomorrow. This is what a pro cyclist really eats on the biggest training day of the week."

**[0:30 – SETUP]**
> "I'm [Name], I race for [Team]. Today's plan: 6 hours, 180 km, with two 30-minute threshold efforts. I'll show you every single thing I eat, how many carbs, why, and what you can steal for your own rides. Even if you're riding 2 hours, not 6."

**[CHAPTER 1 – Breakfast (2:00)]**
- Show the cooking. Explain the "carb loading the morning of" approach: porridge, white bread, honey. Low fiber before big rides (why: gut comfort).
- Graphic: macros of breakfast.
- **Tip for amateurs:** "Eat 2 to 3 hours before. If you ride early, a small carb snack is better than nothing."

**[CHAPTER 2 – Pocket prep (4:00)]**
- Lay out everything. Explain carbs per hour. Show a homemade rice cake recipe (an engaging mini-segment).
- "Most amateurs eat maybe 30 grams per hour. I'm going for 100. Watch how different my power is at hour 5."

**[CHAPTER 3 – On the bike (5:30)]**
- GoPro: talking while riding. Every 30 minutes a "fuel check" graphic.
- Show the effort, the struggle in the intervals, the cafe stop. "Pro secret: the café stop is planned."
- Hour 5 power vs hour 1 (proof that fueling works).

**[CHAPTER 4 – Recovery window (9:00)]**
- Shake, recovery meal, explanation of the 30 to 60 minute window (keep it simple and don't over-claim the science).

**[CHAPTER 5 – Lunch, snack, dinner (10:00)]**
- Quick cooking montage. Show the realistic stuff (sometimes it's frozen pizza and that's okay).
- Running calorie counter in the corner.

**[PAYOFF (13:00)]**
> "Total: 7,140 calories. And here's the thing: tomorrow morning I'll weigh the same as today. Your body uses what you give it. The biggest mistake I see in amateur riders is fear of food. Fuel your rides and you'll be faster. I promise."

**[OUTRO (14:00)]**
> "If you want to see what I eat on race day, which is completely different, click this video right here. See you."

## Video 2 – "Pro Cyclist Joins Random Group Ride (They Had No Idea)"
**Length:** 12 to 18 min | **Pillar:** Personality | **Thumbnail:** You in plain kit, grinning, a group behind you looking surprised. Text: "THEY HAD NO IDEA"

**[0:00 – COLD OPEN]**
> *(Clip of the reveal moment: someone saying "Wait, you're WHO?!")*
> **SAY (to camera, whispering at the meet-up):** "Today I'm joining a club ride in [city]. No one knows I'm a pro. I'm going to act like a total beginner… until the last climb."

**[SETUP]**
- Explain the rules you set yourself: no team kit, an old bike/neutral bike, and "I only reveal when someone asks or at the top of [climb]".
- Introduce your camera person/friend.

**[CHAPTER 1 – The meet-up]** Funny small talk. Ask beginner questions ("Do I need to bring a pump?"). Capture people's genuine kindness (it makes viewers like the group too).

**[CHAPTER 2 – The flat section]** Sit in the group, show group riding etiquette moments and explain them in VO for viewers ("This is how a proper paceline works…"). That turns it into educational content too.

**[CHAPTER 3 – The tease]** You "accidentally" ride a bit too strong on a small rise. Someone comments. Build tension.

**[CHAPTER 4 – The final climb]** Ride with the strongest rider, chat with ease, then ride away. Use the GoPro facing back.

**[CHAPTER 5 – The reveal]** At the top or the café. Genuine reactions. Do a mini Q&A with the group: their questions about pro life are great content.

**[PAYOFF]** You pay for coffee and give one rider a signed jersey/bottle.
> "Honestly? This was the most fun ride of my year. Club riders are the heart of this sport."

**[OUTRO]** "Comment which city I should go to next, and if you're from there, maybe I'll show up at your ride."

**IMPORTANT:** Get written/verbal consent from everyone shown on camera, or blur them.

## Video 3 – "Race Day Vlog: Inside a Pro Race (From 6 a.m. to Bedtime)"
**Length:** 15 to 25 min | **Pillar:** Pro Life Access | **Thumbnail:** Your dirty, exhausted face after the finish. Text: "186 KM OF PAIN"

**[COLD OPEN]**
> *(Finish line footage or the hardest moment of the race, your heavy breathing)*
> **VO:** "186 kilometers, 3,200 meters of climbing and one moment that decided everything. This is what a pro race day really looks like."

**[CHAPTERS]**
1. **Wake-up & breakfast** – what you eat 3 hours before. Your nerves. Room-mate cameo.
2. **The team bus** – a quick tour (seats, kitchen, shower, washing machine). The mechanics working.
3. **Team meeting** – blur the whiteboard, but explain your role today ("I'm working for [leader] until the last climb").
4. **Warm-up & sign-on** – fans, autographs, nervous chat with other riders.
5. **The race** – use your bike cam (if allowed), team-car footage or TV clips (careful: you can't use TV footage without permission. Use your own or ask the organizer/team media). Talk through the race in VO afterwards: "At km 90 the crosswinds hit, and this is where it got chaotic…"
6. **Post-race** – the bus, a shower, recovery shake, the feelings (be honest, especially if it went badly).
7. **Data breakdown** – put your power file on screen. "My normalized power was X, the hardest 5 minutes were Y." Data fans love this.
8. **Evening** – massage, dinner, the team debrief, sleep.

**[PAYOFF]** Your result + the lesson.
> "I finished [position], but my job was to get [leader] to the bottom of the last climb in position. And we did. That's pro cycling: sometimes your best day doesn't show in the results."

**Tip:** Make it a recurring series: "Race Vlog #1, #2…". One per race block keeps your channel alive during the season.

## Video 4 – "Pro vs Amateur: We Rode the Same 3 Climbs"
**Length:** 12 to 16 min | **Pillar:** Training & Data | **Thumbnail:** Split image of you and the amateur, both suffering, with "PRO vs YOU" and the time gap "+7:12"

**Concept:** Bring in 1 to 3 amateurs at different levels (a beginner, a strong club rider, a strong female rider). Do 3 tests:
1. **A 1-minute sprint-style effort** (power + speed)
2. **A 10-minute climb** (time + watts + heart rate)
3. **"Can you hold my wheel?"** – you ride at your endurance pace, and they try to stay on

**Script beats:**
- **Hook:** "How much faster is a pro cyclist than a good amateur? Not on paper. On the road."
- **Setup:** Introduce each rider with their stats (FTP, weight, years riding). Make them likeable.
- **Test 1, 2, 3:** Graphics comparing W/kg side by side. Their reactions. Your tips to each rider on the spot (that's the value).
- **Twist:** In one test you give them a handicap (a head start, or you ride with a heavy backpack). Viewers love the drama.
- **Payoff:** The final scoreboard + the key lessons: "The biggest difference isn't the peak power. It's how long we can repeat it."
- **CTA:** "Comment your FTP and weight. I'll tell you how far off a pro you really are." (comment explosion)

## Video 5 – "The Truth About Being a Pro Cyclist (Money, Pressure & What Nobody Tells You)"
**Length:** 15 to 20 min | **Pillar:** Real Talk | **Format:** Sit-down talk, good light, B-roll inserts | **Thumbnail:** Serious face, text: "THE TRUTH"

**Outline:**
1. **Hook:** "Everyone thinks being a pro cyclist is the dream. And in a way, it is. But there's a side of this job nobody shows you."
2. **The money** – salary ranges (public info), bonuses, prize money split with the team, the cost of being a pro (coaching, food, travel, sometimes paying for your own equipment at lower levels).
3. **The time** – 200+ days away from home, missing birthdays and weddings.
4. **The body** – weight pressure, crashes, illness, the constant tiredness. (Be careful and responsible with weight topics: talk about health, not numbers.)
5. **The mental side** – contract stress (often 1 to 2 year deals), what happens at the end of a contract year.
6. **Why I still love it** – the best moments, the freedom, the views, the brotherhood.
7. **Why I'm making YouTube** – the honest reason: building something for after my career. Invite people on the journey.
8. **Outro:** "If you've ever dreamed of being a pro, comment your question below. I'll answer the best ones in a Q&A video."

This kind of video builds the strongest loyalty and the highest subscriber conversion.

## Video 6 – "My Hardest Workout of the Year (Can You Survive It?)"
**Length:** 10 to 14 min | **Pillar:** Training | **Thumbnail:** Your face in agony on the trainer, sweat. Text: "CAN YOU SURVIVE?"

**Script:**
- **Hook:** "My coach calls this the 'Destroyer'. 4 x 8 minutes at threshold with 30-second sprints every 2 minutes. Today you're doing it with me."
- **Explain the workout** with a simple graphic (zones, durations). Give a scaled amateur version as a % of FTP: "If your FTP is 250, here are your numbers."
- **Do it on camera** with real-time talk (it will get harder to talk, and that's the content). A power graph overlay.
- **Between intervals:** a short coaching tip (breathing, cadence, mindset).
- **After:** lying on the floor, honest reaction, the data review.
- **Value drop:** "When to do this workout, and when NOT to (never in the week before an event)."
- **CTA:** "Try it and tag me on Instagram. I'll repost the best suffering faces." (cross-platform growth)
- **Monetization:** Offer the workout as a free downloadable file (.zwo / TrainingPeaks), which builds your email list (see file 07).
