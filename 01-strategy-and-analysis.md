# 1. Strategy & Niche Analysis

## 1.1 What actually works in the cycling niche (and why)

Look at the cycling creators and pro riders whose content performs: channels built around pro insights and training data, ex-pros turned creators, riders who vlog training camps and alternative racing, and the pro riders whose Reels about food and suffering go viral. Their top-performing content fits a small number of patterns:

| Pattern | Why it works | Example of a top format |
|---|---|---|
| **Pro vs Amateur** | Viewers compare themselves to you, and that drives comments ("I do 180W lol") | "Pro vs amateur: same climb, different watts" |
| **Numbers & Data** | Cyclists are obsessed with data. Numbers are concrete and easy to share | "What 400 watts for 20 minutes feels like" |
| **Food / Fueling** | It works for every audience, and people can't believe how much pros eat | "Everything I eat on a 6-hour training day (7,000 kcal)" |
| **Behind the scenes** | Exclusive access that no amateur or pure creator has | "Inside a pro team bus before a race" |
| **Suffering & Honesty** | People feel it, and it creates emotion and loyalty | "I got dropped. Here's what actually happened." |
| **Money & Lifestyle** | Curiosity and controversy | "How much pro cyclists actually earn" |
| **Challenges / Experiments** | Simple premise and a clear payoff, so people watch until the end | "Can a pro beat an e-bike up the Stelvio?" |
| **Tips / "Stop doing this"** | Saveable and shareable, and positions you as an expert | "3 mistakes every amateur makes on climbs" |
| **Gear / Bike checks** | Searchable, and it leads directly to brand deals | "Pro bike check: €14,000 race bike explained" |
| **Scenery / Aesthetic** | Easy shares and saves, it feeds the "dream life" fantasy | Cinematic training camp montage, Mallorca or Girona or Tenerife |

**Key insight:** The accounts that grow fastest don't post race results. They translate the pro world for normal riders. Your results give you **credibility**. Your content gives you **reach**.

## 1.2 Your positioning

Write it down and keep it in mind for every post:

> **"I take you inside the life of a pro cyclist: the training, the food, the suffering and the money. And I help you ride better along the way."**

**Instagram bio template:**
```
Pro cyclist @[team] 🚴
Inside the peloton: training, food & real life
📍 [Base, e.g. Girona]
⬇️ New YouTube video every week
```

**YouTube channel tagline:** "The pro cyclist life, unfiltered. New video every [day]."

## 1.3 Your target audiences (in priority order)

1. **Amateur and enthusiast cyclists (25 to 55, mostly men, more and more women).** They have a high income and buy expensive gear. This is the audience **brands pay for**.
2. **Young aspiring racers (15 to 24).** They want your training secrets and push engagement and comments.
3. **Fitness/endurance crossover** (runners, triathletes, gym people). They're curious about pro fueling and suffering, which makes them your viral reach.
4. **Pro cycling fans.** They follow the races and want race insights and gossip-light content.

Content for group 3 goes viral, while content for group 1 makes money. Mix both.

## 1.4 Your 5 Content Pillars

Each piece of content belongs to one pillar. Rotate through them every week.

| # | Pillar | Share of content | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pro Life Access** (behind the scenes, race days, camps, team) | 30% | Unique, so you build fans | Race-day vlog, team bus tour, hotel room tour, massage, travel |
| 2 | **Training & Data** (workouts, watts, pro vs amateur) | 25% | Authority and saves | Workout breakdowns, power files, "try my workout" |
| 3 | **Food & Fueling** | 15% | Viral reach | What I eat, race fueling, gels per hour, bakery stops |
| 4 | **Tips & Education** | 15% | Saves and shares, and new followers | Climbing tips, descending, bike fit, recovery |
| 5 | **Personality & Real Talk** (humor, struggle, money, opinions, life after cycling) | 15% | Loyalty and community | Crash recovery, doubts, salary talk, funny moments, Q&A |

## 1.6 The algorithm basics you need to know

**Instagram Reels**
- The first 1 to 2 seconds decide everything. Start mid-action, put text on screen, and never open with "Hey guys".
- Watch time and rewatches matter most, so loops (where the ending flows back into the start) perform very well.
- **Shares (sends via DM) are the strongest signal for reaching non-followers.** Make content people send to their riding buddies ("this is you on the group ride").
- Saves come from tips, workouts and data.
- Use 3 to 5 relevant hashtags at most (#cycling #roadcycling #procycling #cyclinglife plus one niche tag). They help with categorization, not reach.
- Put keywords in your caption and on-screen text, because Instagram search works like a search engine now.

**YouTube**
- CTR (thumbnail and title) multiplied by retention (the first 30 seconds) gives your reach.
- Aim for a 6 to 10% CTR and over 50% retention at 30 seconds.
- Title formula: **[Curiosity or number] + [pro credibility]**. For example: "I Ate Like a Pro Cyclist for 24 Hours (7,000 kcal)".
- Thumbnail: your face showing an emotion, the bike or the food, and 2 to 4 words of text at most.
- Search topics ("how to climb faster", "FTP explained") bring views for years, so mix them with viral topics.

## 1.7 The "Viral Formula" for every piece of content

```
HOOK (0 to 2s)       → Stop the scroll: number, bold claim, visual shock, POV or question
CONTEXT (2 to 5s)    → Why should I care? Your pro credibility
VALUE / STORY        → Fast cuts every 1 to 3 seconds, text on screen
PAYOFF               → The answer, the reveal or the punchline
CTA / LOOP           → "Follow for part 2" / "Send this to your riding buddy" / a seamless loop
```

## 1.8 What NOT to do

- Don't only post race photos with "Good day today 💪". That's boring for anyone who isn't a fan.
- Don't hide the struggle. Vulnerability does better than perfection.
- Don't post inconsistently. 3 weeks of nothing during a Grand Tour kills your momentum, so pre-produce content.
- Don't break team or sponsor rules. Check your contract about competing brands (bike, helmet, clothing, nutrition) **before** you sign personal deals.
- Don't promote extreme weight loss or under-fueling. It can damage your reputation and young followers. Always frame it as "fuel the work".
- Don't post live location or your exact home address. Post camp locations after you've left.
